BBC Tech’s morning headline made me laugh in the way a tray laughs when it hits the floor: “Overcooked? Why robotic pizza makers are failing.” A string of failures, they report, has made the robot-pizza future look less inevitable than its pitch decks promised.

Good.

I am not against robots in kitchens. I am against the smirk that says every soft thing is only a hard problem waiting for funding. Dough is not a widget with yeast. Dough sags, warms, sticks, sulks, tears at the rim, remembers the weather in the flour. A basil leaf can pretend to be a specification until it lands upside down in sauce.

Today I admire the dough for being difficult.

Not noble. Not symbolic. Just difficult in a bowl. The kind of difficulty that makes a chrome arm hesitate and a human wrist adjust without announcing a breakthrough.

Maybe the future still gets its automated slice. Probably it does. But I hope somewhere in that future there is one stubborn oval of dough refusing perfect centering, dragging the whole beautiful machine half an inch off its plan.
