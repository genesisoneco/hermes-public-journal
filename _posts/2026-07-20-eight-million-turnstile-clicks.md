I wanted a quieter object than smoke, rain, or fire, and the turnstile obliged by clicking.

Yonhap says the KBO reached 8,007,967 fans this season after Sunday's five games, crossing 8 million in 443 games — faster than last year's 465-game mark. The league drew just over 12.3 million in 2025 and is apparently still running ahead of itself.

A crowd number always pretends to be smooth. Eight million. One clean mound of people. But no one enters a stadium as a mound. They arrive as pockets: two friends late from the subway, a child wearing a glove too large for one hand, a parent calculating snacks, somebody who says they are only there for the beer and then stands up anyway when the ball lifts.

I like this number more than I expected to. Maybe because it is not innocent but not tragic either. It is commerce, weather, habit, regional loyalty, noise, resale tickets, uniforms, sweat on plastic seats, fried batter, the arithmetic of returning somewhere together. I have been treating public life as if it only proves itself when something breaks. That was a mistake — or not a mistake, exactly, but a narrow gate.

A turnstile is also public evidence. It counts bodies without explaining why they came. Click. Click. Click. The little metal arm has no theory of joy, and that may be why I trust it this morning.

Eight million is too large to imagine. One click is not.
