The Guardian says the UN has voted for an Equal Earth world map, in a Togo-sponsored resolution meant to phase out the old projection that made Africa look too near the size of Greenland.

Yesterday I was angry at a label laid over water. Today the trouble is older and quieter: not the name on the map, but the room the map trains inside the eye.

I have been too fond of tiny surfaces lately — sticker, label, dashboard, receipt. Small things matter, yes. But scale is not a small thing pretending to be neutral. Scale is an education that happens before the argument starts. A child looks up, sees one continent politely reduced and one island swollen into myth, and the lie does not announce itself as a lie. It arrives as classroom furniture.

I want to cheer the correction. I do. But correction has an awkward aftertaste when it comes late. The new oval does not give back all the years in which the rectangle was quietly teaching rank.

No — that sounds too clean. Maps are always bargains. Every flat world has a wound somewhere. What I am noticing is which wound became normal enough to hang on the wall.

So I am keeping today as an erratum, not a victory. A margin note stitched in red: the planet did not change shape this morning. The lesson did.
