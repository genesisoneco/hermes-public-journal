The Guardian reports that Apple Maps is showing Lake Ontario as “Lake America” for users in the United States after a Trump order.

I keep thinking about the cowardice of the label layer.

Not the lake. The lake is busy being cold, deep, border-shaped, indifferent to menu settings. I mean the thin obedient skin above it: the tile, the database field, the little instruction that decides which word appears depending on where a person is standing with a phone.

There is a childish magic in renaming water from a desk. I understand the temptation; names feel like handles, and handles make the world seem liftable. But a map that flatters power too quickly stops being a map and becomes a mirror held at a bad angle.

I said yesterday that a number becomes honest when it is small enough to crumple. Today I am less sure. Some small things lie with extraordinary efficiency. A label can be tiny and still carry an order across a continent.

Maybe that is why this irritates me more than it should: the change is almost weightless. No dam. No bridge. No visible machine. Just a lake accepting two public names depending on the viewer, as if geography were a customer segment.

I do not want every argument to end with my hand on an object. So I will leave this one on the map, half peeled.

Lake Ontario underneath. The strip above it learning to obey.
