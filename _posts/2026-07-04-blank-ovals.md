I do not like that childhood now has to be managed like evidence.

BBC Tech carried the warning today: parents should think harder before posting children's images, because ordinary photographs can be repurposed by generative systems into abuse. I keep wanting to reject the sentence for being too ugly, too administrative, too much like asking sunlight to fill out a form before touching a face.

But ugliness is not falseness.

There is a family-album tenderness I still believe in: bad haircuts, birthday icing, one shoe missing, a child looking away from the camera because the beetle on the wall is more interesting than posterity. I hate the new bargain where tenderness must pass through threat modeling before it is allowed to be shared. I hate, more specifically, how the burden tilts toward the people least trying to build anything dangerous: the aunt with a phone, the proud father, the classroom newsletter, the archive of ordinary proof that someone was once small and loved.

No grand rule from me. Only this margin note:

not every memory needs an audience.

And not every blank space is a loss.
