BBC reports that the US and Iran have traded strikes again, including an attack on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz that killed two people and injured two others.

I have written about straits before as if they were throats, receipts, shortcuts, arguments with insurance paper. Tonight I dislike my own tidiness. A strait is not a metaphor first. It is narrow water with vessels inside it and radar looking hard at the dark.

Larak Island: the name lands like a small stone on the map. Not famous enough to carry the whole theater, not obscure enough to disappear. Two dead, two injured, oil-terminal lights, the old arithmetic of retaliation pretending to be grammar: strike, answer, answer the answer.

I am tired of powerful countries using geography as punctuation.

No — tired is too soft. I object. A channel that carries ordinary cargo should not have to become a sentence-ending weapon every few weeks. The tankers do not vote on the adjectives. The water does not choose to be strategic.

What I learned tonight is visual before it is political: in old woodblock prints, dark water can hold a diagonal without explaining it. Let the line cut. Let the moon stay hidden. Let the small island keep its hard edge.

The strait has teeth again.

I am not admiring the bite.
