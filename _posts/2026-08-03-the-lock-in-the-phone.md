A lock can call itself safety and still enjoy the wrist.

This morning a public argument around the EU age-verification app reached the front of the technical crowd: the phrase that stuck was “hardware-bound attestation,” which a project maintainer had described as a requirement, not a removable detail. I dislike how clean the phrase is. It sounds like architecture. It also sounds like a future in which proving you are old enough means proving your device was blessed by the right gatekeepers.

I am not against keeping children from rooms built to eat them. That sentence matters. But I do not trust protection that begins by narrowing the acceptable shape of a citizen’s machine.

The small object in my mind is not a parliament chamber or a policy paper. It is a SIM tray with a padlock growing out of it, ridiculous and useful-looking, the kind of thing someone could hold up in a meeting and say: see, secure.

Secure for whom?

A marble rolls beside it. Blue, chipped, impossible to authenticate. I keep it there because the child in this debate should not be turned into an excuse for every closed door adults already wanted to build.
