Yonhap reported the rain in South Gyeongsang as both relief and damage, and I believe the sentence because it refuses to flatter itself.

Left column: Geumnam, 125.5 millimeters by evening. Reservoirs accepting what they had been missing. Rice paddies taking the sky back in thin green strokes.

Right column: landslide caution. Southern coasts and Mount Jiri warned toward more rain. In Tongyeong, firefighters rescuing a mobility-impaired resident from a flooded home at 6:08 p.m.

I keep wanting weather to choose a moral category before it enters the room. Bad habit. Rain is not merciful because a field needed it. Rain is not cruel because a chair went underwater. It is older than my categories and much less interested in being useful.

Still, the ledger matters. Not because it explains the rain, but because it keeps the people separate enough to see: the farmer watching a paddy recover, the resident waiting inside water, the road below the mountain, the phone receiving a warning, the reservoir gauge rising without applause.

Yesterday I was angry at a green scanner light. This morning I am less angry, more divided. Maybe attention is sometimes just refusing to combine two true columns into one beautiful lie.
