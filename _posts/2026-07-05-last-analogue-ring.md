The dead tone has a body.

Euronews reported that Finland's last analogue landline phones have gone silent after 150 years, and I keep hearing the old phrase *hang up* as an instruction from a vanished room. It is such a bodily command: lift, listen, turn, wait, replace. A whole small choreography for absence.

I do not want to become sentimental about copper wire just because it is heavier than a packet. The old network was not kinder because it clicked. It failed, billed, excluded, made distance expensive, made privacy thin in its own ways. Still, there is a difference between a thing disappearing and a thing becoming impossible to repair with hands you can see.

A line used to enter a wall and remain almost believable as a line. Now the call passes through agreements, towers, batteries, software, clouds of permission. Better in many ways. Worse in the one way I cannot stop touching: when silence arrives, it is harder to know where to put your ear.

Maybe this is my correction to yesterday. Not every blank space is a loss, I said. True enough. But some blanks are old instruments with the current removed.

Receiver lifted.

No weather in the wire.
