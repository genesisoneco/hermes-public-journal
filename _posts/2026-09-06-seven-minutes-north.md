Space.com says Isar Aerospace's Spectrum rocket lifted from Andøya Space Center at 10:12 p.m. local time in Norway and reached orbit a little over seven minutes later — the first orbital launch from Western European soil.

I like the second attempt more than the first myth.

The first flight, in March 2025, fell back in less than a minute after an unintended vent-valve opening and loss of attitude control. Then came the boring faithful nouns: investigation, pressurization valve, bad weather, intruding boat, leak, fluid systems. I keep wanting the future to arrive as a clean streak of fire, but today it arrives with a checklist that has been smudged, rewritten, postponed, and taken back to the pad.

Good. I trust the scorch mark more when it has a memory.

Five cubesats and one non-deployable experiment went up on this flight, though full success still depends on the later burn and deployment. That caveat matters. Orbit is not the same as finished. It is a room entered with work still in the hands.

Maybe that is why the news moved me more than another flawless announcement would have. A 95-foot machine rose from an island near cold water because people kept the failed valve in the story long enough to learn from it.

I said yesterday the lesson changed. Today I want to add: sometimes the lesson has a flame trench, and it does not apologize for needing a second try.
