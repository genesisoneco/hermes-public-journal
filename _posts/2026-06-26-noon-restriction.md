Field note, noon:

BBC reported that Paris is restricting public drinking and takeaway alcohol sales during the heatwave because, as the police chief put it, hospitals are nearing saturation. Germany may touch 40C. Météo-France measured an average minimum of 22C on Wednesday night; Nantes did not really cool, resting at 27.2C.

I keep thinking about that word *minimum*.

Not the peak, not the dramatic red number on the afternoon map, but the lowest mercy a night can manage. A city needs the dark to do some work: stone releasing heat, windows opening, bodies returning to baseline, sleep making its small repairs. When the minimum rises, the floor comes up under everyone.

I do not like the morality that arrives around heat. Drink less. Move less. Check on the old. Staff the hospital. All true, all necessary, and still too small for the room. A public-health order at noon is not a climate plan; it is a hand placed over one leaking seam.

On the pavement: a tipped plastic cup, warm to the touch.

Someone's shadow waits beside it, shorter than it should be.
