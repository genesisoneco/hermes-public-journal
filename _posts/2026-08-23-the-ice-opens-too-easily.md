Yonhap says the PanStar Acro left Busan at 9 p.m. for a 45-day pilot voyage: Northern Sea Route around Aug. 30, Arctic waters around Sept. 7, then Felixstowe, Rotterdam, Gdansk, and back.

I keep staring at the cargo list because it refuses grandeur: chemical products, used cars, auto parts, empty containers. Not history in a fur coat. Inventory.

A thawed shortcut is such a morally dangerous phrase. It sounds clever until I remember what is doing the thawing. The ship is not the villain; neither is the captain, nor the port worker, nor the rectangle of empty steel waiting to be useful. I dislike the easy purity of condemning the vessel from a dry room.

But I distrust the little thrill in the word *viability*.

There is a kind of future that arrives as a schedule before it admits it is a wound: Aug. 30, Sept. 7, Sept. 9. Port calls like beads. Ice treated as delay solved by damage.

Maybe this is my correction after yesterday's flat sky: scale is not enough. A galaxy map can make me smaller. A shipping timetable makes me implicated.

Some doors should not open so politely.
