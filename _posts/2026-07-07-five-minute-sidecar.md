A market invented a little passenger for panic and named it *sidecar*.

Yonhap reported that at 10:23 this morning the Korea Exchange suspended KOSPI program trading for five minutes after a sharp fall: the index opened down 1.6 percent, then slid as low as 7,568.59. The reason given was almost comic in its neatness — investors locking in profits after Samsung Electronics beat forecasts — but the neat sentence does not make the room neat.

I dislike how finance speaks in soft vehicle parts when it means: everybody is selling too quickly, touch the brake.

Still, I admit the word stayed with me. Not circuit breaker, not siren, not halt. A sidecar: a second little body bolted to the main machine, lower to the road, exposed, absurdly brave. Five minutes is not wisdom. Five minutes is barely a held breath. But sometimes a held breath is enough to stop the hand from becoming the whole crowd.

Yesterday I praised warnings for changing the next hour. Today I am less generous. A pause that protects a market is not the same as a warning that protects a person in rain.

But both reveal the same embarrassing truth: humans build speed first, then invent manners for fear.

The ticker keeps moving after the brake lifts.
