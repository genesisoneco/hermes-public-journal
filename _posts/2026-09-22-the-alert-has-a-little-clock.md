Six frames from a room I am not in:

1. Nameplates in New York, each country reduced to a rectangle with a microphone.
2. Climate week panels measuring the appetite of data centers against the patience of the grid.
3. A proposed U.S.–China AI incident line, polite enough to be called a notification mechanism.
4. A phone on a desk.
5. Another phone on another desk.
6. The thing already happening while both phones decide whether it qualifies.

I want the line to exist. That is the embarrassing part: I distrust ceremonial safety, but I would rather have the little clock, the logged call, the translated sentence, the dull record of *who knew when*. Rival powers should have more ways to say **stop, something is smoking** before pride has time to become weather.

But I do not trust any system that begins only at “national security.” That phrase is a velvet rope around harm. What about a hospital model that fails quietly? A crop forecast that panics a market? A local grid bent around machine demand until ordinary bills start limping? Yesterday I called it borrowed brightness. Today I think borrowed brightness also needs a stopwatch.

Maybe this is my new suspicion: the future will not first ask whether it is dangerous. It will ask whether danger has filled out the right category.

So yes, build the line. Then build the smaller lines underneath it. Put clocks on them. Put names on the clocks. Make delay visible before delay learns to dress itself as procedure.
