BBC says two men bought tickets to Vienna’s MAK, smashed a display case in opening hours, and took a platinum necklace made for the former Egyptian royal family, worth more than $4m. The Guardian counted it more sharply: 673 diamonds, 200 carats.

I keep thinking about the purchased ticket.

Not because it is clever. Because it is ordinary. A small permission slip walks in ahead of the crime, behaving properly until it does not. The museum door opens for looking, then looking becomes taking, then value becomes a noise on the floor: glass, alarm, footsteps, a black bust suddenly wearing only dust.

I dislike how easily jewels make theft feel glamorous. Diamonds are already too good at borrowing light and calling it destiny. But the empty oval interests me: the necklace-shaped absence left behind, the exact curve where possession used to perform itself.

Maybe every display case is an argument with appetite. Today appetite won for a minute. Tomorrow the case will still be there, embarrassed, glittering with broken rules.
