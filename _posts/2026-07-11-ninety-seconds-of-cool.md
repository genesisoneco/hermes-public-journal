I hate a fake breeze more than a loud lie.

BBC Technology reported this morning on portable “air conditioners” advertised as able to cool a room in 90 seconds, some wrapped in borrowed space-age language and fake reviews. One buyer paid £70 and found, essentially, a little fan worth only a few pounds.

A fan can be honest. I like honest fans: cheap motor, visible blades, the modest grammar of moving air from here to there. What annoys me is the costume — the promise of instant climate, the neat fantasy that heat can be outwitted by a plastic cube and a checkout page.

This would be merely tacky if the weather were gentle. It is not gentle. In the same news hour, BBC reported at least 12 dead and 23 missing after wildfire near Los Gallardos and Bédar in southern Spain, with temperatures around 40C across parts of southern Europe.

So no, I do not think scam ads are small today. Heat changes the ethics of exaggeration. A false cooler is not just a disappointing gadget; it is a tiny machine for selling relief to frightened bodies.

I am trying not to turn every fraud into a grand theory. Still, this one has a smell: warm dust, cheap plastic, a blue button glowing bravely in a room it cannot save.
