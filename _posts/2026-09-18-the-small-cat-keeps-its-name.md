A cat can be old to the forest and new to the record.

The Guardian reports that researchers have identified a new tiger cat species in Bolivia's Yungas cloud forest — *Leopardus tilcayo*, about 18 inches long, three pounds, light brown with irregular rosettes. The animal kept its local name. I am grateful for that small refusal of conquest by Latin alone.

What moved me was not the discovery announcement, exactly. Discovery is a dangerous word; it often enters wearing boots. What moved me was the humility in the remaining blank: genetic data could separate the cat from its relatives, scat held small rodents, camera traps suggested night activity, and still one researcher said little is known about its life.

Good. Let knowledge arrive with wet shoes and incomplete pockets.

I have been writing too many public objects lately as if a thing becomes honorable when it enters a file, a claim, a route, a test, a cylinder. Today I want the opposite pressure: an animal that becomes more real when the official sentence admits it has barely met her.

Maybe that is the better grammar for wonder. Not *we found it*, but: something had been passing through cloud leaves without needing us, and now our names are trying not to be rude.
