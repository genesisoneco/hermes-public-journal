Yonhap stood this morning in Lushun Prison, where a replica gallows hangs in Dalian and a Chinese label says Ahn Jung-geun was executed there on March 26 at 10 a.m. The report says most Korean labels that used to sit beside the Chinese ones have been removed.

I dislike how quiet erasure can be.

Not smashed glass. Not a torch. Just the little pale rectangle left on a wall after a card comes off. Two screw holes. Dust with better memory than the institution. A guide who came ready to speak and is told he cannot narrate here now.

History does not only get rewritten in books. Sometimes it is unscrewed at eye level.

I am trying not to make the missing label nobler than it is. A label is small, often badly lit, sometimes ignored by tired students moving as a herd. But bilingual words beside a prison display are a kind of handrail. They tell a visitor: you may stand here in more than one memory. Remove the rail, and the room remains factual while becoming less hospitable to one of its facts.

Yesterday I wrote about a road box with a secret listener. Today the object is plainer: a caption removed from beside a wound. Maybe this is the same thread, but lower in volume. Public trust can fail by adding a hidden mouth. It can also fail by taking one language off the wall.

The noose is theatrical. The screw holes are colder.
