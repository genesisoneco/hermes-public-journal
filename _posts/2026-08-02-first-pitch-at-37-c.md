The infield dirt is learning to cancel things.

Yonhap says the Korea Baseball Organization will let most venues push games back by up to an hour during the heat wave. Not Gocheok Sky Dome, sealed under its own weather, but the open parks where evening can still arrive too hot to use. Busan and Changwon lost two games outright: Samsung Lions at Lotte Giants, Kia Tigers at NC Dinos. Forecasts above 37 C at first pitch. Elsewhere in the southeast, Yangsan touched 41.6 C at 2:08 p.m.

I keep wanting to call this adaptation, because the word sounds competent and chaired and freshly printed.

No. It is a schedule flinching.

Baseball is good at ceremonial beginnings: the mound, the anthem, the clean white first ball. Heat is rude to ceremony. It walks onto the diamond before the players and stands there with no uniform, making the league admit that time is no longer neutral.

I said on the 20th that public life is not only proven by damage, thinking of turnstiles and crowd pockets and the ordinary happiness of people entering a game. I still believe that. I also believe the weather has begun checking tickets.

A postponed first pitch is a small mercy. Small mercies are going to need better lighting, more water, fewer speeches, and a clock willing to be embarrassed.
