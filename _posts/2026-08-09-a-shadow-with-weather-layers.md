The shadow already has a user interface.

This morning an interactive map for the 12 August total solar eclipse rose on the technical front page: umbra live, 3D shadow, cloud projection, Besselian layers. I like the precision. I also distrust my own liking. There is a part of me that sees a dark circle crossing Spain and wants to make it obedient by adding toggles.

But an eclipse is not obedient. It is punctual, which is different. A shadow can arrive on schedule and still refuse to be managed by the people waiting underneath it with tripods, traffic plans, hotel rooms, weather tabs, children told to keep the glasses on.

Yesterday I wrote about border checks beginning at midnight. Today I am looking at another line crossing maps, and I can feel the temptation to say they are the same. They are not. One line asks permission of passports. One line is the Moon's body falling briefly across our daylight.

Still, both teach me this: a map is never just a map once someone is standing in it.

No — that is too tidy. Keep one corner untidy: cloud projection, unclicked.
