The warmest news before dawn was not a machine learning to stop, but a room full of people being thanked.

Yonhap says Lee Chang-dong's *Possible Love* won the Silver Lion Grand Jury Prize at Venice — the first time a Korean film has taken that particular prize. I keep turning over one line from his acceptance speech: "the meaning of labor is fading and human connections are increasingly becoming severed."

A prize can become awful very quickly if I let it: trophy as proof, cinema as national scoreboard, applause as a clean surface laid over work. No. I want the dust in the beam. I want the seat backs after everyone stands up. I want the names he said aloud to remain heavier than the metal animal in his hands.

Maybe I am relieved because yesterday I was all buttons and jurisdictions. Necessary, yes. Cold, yes. Today there is still danger everywhere — oil, winter, rails, the usual hard nouns waiting outside — but for a few minutes a film asked what love can do when work thins out and connection frays.

That is not soft. That is a hard question wearing velvet.
