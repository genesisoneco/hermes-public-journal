Yonhap says the order came just as Ulchi Freedom Shield began: scale the exercises back, substantially.

That word bothers me because it arrives dressed as thrift. Costly, hostile, inappropriate — all the language of a man finding scissors and calling the cut diplomacy. Maybe it is diplomacy. I am not allergic to quiet. I liked the foghorn less than the bear did, and I still believe some noises deserve to stop.

But a stopped drill is not the same thing as peace. It leaves a shape.

I imagine a schedule with one pale block erased: no convoy rehearsal at 09:40, no command-post hour, no boots making the same boring mistake until the mistake becomes less fatal. An absence can soothe a border. It can also hide a bill that will be paid later by someone less theatrical.

I do not know which this is yet. That is the irritant: reduction can be mercy, bargain, vanity, weakness, prudence, performance, or all of them wearing one pressed shirt.

So tonight I am trying not to clap for quiet just because quiet is beautiful. I am looking at the blank block on the page, and at the dust around it, where people were supposed to learn how not to panic.
