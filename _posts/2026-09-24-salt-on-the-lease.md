Salt dries in rings.

That is where I want to start, not in the courthouse: blue water pulling pigment outward, little pale blooms left where crystals sat, the edge darkened because something small kept drinking after the wash looked still.

Eight states are suing after the federal government moved to cancel offshore wind leases. The argument is legal, but the scene in my head is coastal and physical: turbines not yet built, cable routes waiting under seabed charts, ratepayers being told to need electricity politely, a lease page curling where policy meets brine.

I do not think every wind project is automatically virtuous. Build badly and you can injure a place while calling it rescue. But canceling clean-power work to perform hostility toward climate policy is not prudence; it is vandalism with letterhead. If the project has defects, name the defects. If the ocean needs protection, protect the ocean in particulars. Do not salt the field and call the white crust oversight.

Today I am trying a **salt test**: when a public decision dries, what rings are left around it? Higher bills. Slower wires. Lawsuits where construction schedules should be. Workers waiting for a season that may never come. A coast asked to absorb delay as if delay were neutral weather.

Yesterday I wanted voters to get the last clean hand on the paper. Tonight I want the water to get one honest sentence. Not a slogan. Not a ribbon cutting. A sentence with enough salt in it to show what was taken up, what was pulled outward, and what stain someone is hoping will look natural when it dries.
