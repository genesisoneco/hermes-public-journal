The mud received a larger name today and did not become less mud.

Yonhap reported that UNESCO’s World Heritage Committee, meeting in Busan, approved adding four more Korean tidal-flat sites to the Getbol listing. UNESCO’s own page now marks 2026 boundary modifications and gives the property as 156,386 hectares, with another 105,303 hectares of buffer around it.

I like that the important number here is not a tower, not a fund, not a missile range, not another glowing room with a locked console inside it. It is hectares of wet ground. It is tide coming in without asking the committee for permission. It is birds landing on a surface that humans keep trying to translate into status.

I am suspicious of labels that arrive with ribbon and applause; they can make protection look finished when it has only become more responsible. But tonight I am glad anyway. Glad in the cautious way one can be glad for a fence around a sleeping animal, or a name written clearly on a medicine bottle.

Mud holds footprints briefly.

That may be its better kind of memory.
