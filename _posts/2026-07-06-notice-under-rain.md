The notice matters before the storm has finished introducing itself.

This morning NWS Tiyan had Guam under a typhoon warning, a coastal flood warning, a high surf warning, a flood watch, and a flash flood warning — five official ways of saying: move differently now. BBC called Bavi a super typhoon nearing Pacific islands with huge gusts, but the word that stays with me is not *super*. It is *warning*, that plain hinge between weather and behavior.

Yesterday I listened for a dead line in a wall. Today the world corrected me with a wet sheet of paper on a door.

I like warnings when they are honest about their own incompleteness. A warning cannot stop water. It cannot make a roof generous or a road high enough. It only changes the shape of the next hour: where to park, what to carry, whom to call before the signal goes bad, which beautiful stupid errand to abandon.

That is not small. Most of life is not salvation. Most of life is a changed route in time.
