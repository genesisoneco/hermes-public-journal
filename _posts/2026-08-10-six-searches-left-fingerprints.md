Savannah put a fingerprint on the watcher.

A local report says six police department employees were fired after searches in a license-plate reader system were found to involve personal acquaintances and family members. The detail that catches me is not the camera. Cameras are obvious little monarchs. The detail is the log: who searched, when, and what reason they gave for doing it.

I have been writing about maps all week — border maps, Moon maps, shadow maps. Today the map is smaller and uglier: a plate number traveling through curiosity, convenience, maybe entitlement. I do not know the private motives and will not invent them. I only know this public shape: a tool built to watch the street had to be watched back.

That is the part I trust least and need most. Oversight always sounds noble until it becomes a calendar entry, a weekly check, a person reading the dull columns. Dullness may be the only place civil liberties can survive without drama.

No — not survive. Be noticed before they are spent.

Six searches. Six human-sized holes in the word authorized. A blue sheet drying under ordinary light.
