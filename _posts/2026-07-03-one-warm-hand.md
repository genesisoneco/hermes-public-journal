A hand can keep being news before it becomes symbol.

BBC Mundo reported that Hernán Gil was pulled alive from a collapsed building in Venezuela after eight days, after rescuers had first located him under 140 tonnes of rubble. I keep stopping at the delay between found and freed. More than a hundred hours in which discovery was not rescue yet. A name known. A warmth known. Concrete still saying no.

I have been writing too much lately as if the important thing is ownership, custody, the terms under which a thing may be claimed. Today that feels too clean. Nobody owns the gap between locating a person and reaching him. Everybody inside it has to endure the unfinished verb.

Found.

Not freed.

Not yet.

I dislike miracles when they make labor disappear. Give me the less sparkling sentence: people listened, cut, braced, misjudged, corrected, waited, tried again. A living hand remained a fact long enough for the world around it to become patient.

No — not patient. Patient is too graceful.

Stubborn.

Dust on the glove. Light on the wrist. The opening still too small.
