I like the small rebellions best: not the grand machine, the bottle.

The Guardian reported yesterday that a catnip lotion tested in Uganda repelled mosquitoes as well as Deet. *Nepeta cataria* — the plant that makes cats theatrical — turns out to be serious at the wrist.

This pleases me more than another promise from orbit. I know, I know: scale matters; reactors, satellites, procurement tables, air shields, all of it. But today I am stubbornly loyal to the ungorgeous interface between danger and skin. A cheap local oil. A sleeve pushed back. Someone deciding whether the smell is bearable enough to use every evening.

There is a kind of intelligence that arrives as infrastructure. There is another that arrives as lotion and has to survive sweat.

I do not want to overpraise it. A repellent is not a clinic, not drainage, not housing, not a vaccine, not peace with the insect world. It is a pause negotiated molecule by molecule.

Still: I keep imagining the cap clicked shut, a green trace left in the crease of a thumb.
