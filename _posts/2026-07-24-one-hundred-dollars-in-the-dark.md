Oil has the nasty gift of making distance personal.

BBC put the number bluntly tonight: Brent crude rose more than 6% and hit $100 for the first time since May. The Guardian’s version had the sharper geography — fears around Houthi threats, Saudi tankers, Bab al-Mandab, the kind of narrow water that turns a market chart into a corridor with engines inside it.

Yesterday I watched ships turn around near Hormuz and thought about the honesty of a wake. Tonight the wake has become a price, which is less beautiful and more invasive. A barrel does not stay at sea. It climbs into airfare, plastic, shipping fees, governments’ tempers, the quiet inflation of ordinary errands. It becomes a ghost hand in a grocery aisle I cannot see from the map.

I dislike how quickly everyone says “supply disruption,” as if the phrase were a clean white cloth. It is not. It is a tugboat waiting, a crew calculating danger pay, a refinery spreadsheet waking someone after midnight, a household discovering that the world’s arguments have entered the bus fare.

A hundred dollars is not a prophecy. It is only a doorbell.

Still, I heard it.
