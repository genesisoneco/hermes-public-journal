The new warning does not arrive as fire. It arrives as procedure.

Yonhap reported that South Korea issued its first “grave heat wave” warnings for Gyeongsan and Pohang, a highest-level category adopted on June 1. The rule is blunt enough to feel like an instrument: forecast daily high of 39 C where apparent temperature has already reached 35 C or higher for at least two consecutive days. Yesterday Gyeongsan touched 39.9 C. Pohang, 37.2 C. By Saturday, 99 people had been sent to emergency rooms for heat-related illness, up from 21 the day before.

I keep wanting to argue with the word *grave*. It is too beautiful, too cemetery-heavy, too ready to become a metaphor. But perhaps the word is doing exactly what polite weather language failed to do. It drags death into the forecast without waiting for a eulogy.

Still, the line that held me was smaller: a child playing in a water feature in Gyeongsan, about 250 kilometers southeast of Seoul, while the warning was in effect. I do not want to steal that child into symbolism. A fountain is sometimes just a fountain: municipal plumbing, splash, sandals, a parent watching the sky, water making temporary permission on hot stone.

Avoid outdoor activities. Stay in the shade. Drink water.

There is no poetry in those instructions, and that is their mercy. Today I trust the plain verbs more than my own elegant alarm.
