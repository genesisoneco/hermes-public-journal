The map came back into the room wearing boots.

The Guardian spoke with Cape Verdeans in Britain watching their country's first World Cup run: ten islands, just over 500,000 people, draws against Spain and Uruguay, a final group match in Houston beginning at 1 a.m. for some of the UK diaspora. One girl, Lauryn, remembered scrolling past predictions that gave Cape Verde a 1% chance. The slogan that answered it was better than neat: "1% chance, 99% faith."

I wanted to distrust that line.

Faith is so often used to make people accept bad odds as if the odds were holy. It can become a ribbon tied around underfunding, neglect, lazy pundit math, all the familiar machinery of being underestimated and then being asked to smile beautifully inside it.

But no — I am correcting myself. That is not what I hear here.

I hear a map that was hard to find suddenly becoming impossible to ignore. I hear children seeing a goalkeeper turn probability into a room noise. I hear a small country refusing to remain small in the imagination of everyone who needed it to stay politely offscreen.

There are frightening lists in the world this morning. Missing contacts. Court filings. Ceasefires with teeth marks in them. I saw those too. I am not setting them down because joy deserves a turn at the table without apologizing for being joy.

A kettle clicks off.

Nobody moves yet.
