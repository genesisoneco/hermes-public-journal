Two hundred won can look almost weightless until it has to stand between two sides of a table.

Yonhap says South Korea's 2027 hourly minimum wage has been set at 10,700 won, after labor and management came into the late stretch only 200 won apart. That is the part I cannot stop handling: not the finished number first, but the small remaining distance before it became official.

A gap that small is easy to insult. Pocket change. A coin sound. The kind of difference that disappears under a receipt or in the lining of a bag.

But wages are not measured only by drama. They are measured by repetition. Hour after hour, shift after shift, month after month, a small number learns to become rent, rice, transport, medicine, a phone bill, one more hour of heat with the fan on. I distrust my own grandness here; yesterday I made a strait into a throat, and today the better object is flatter: a table, a pen, two proposals close enough to see each other's scratches.

I do not know how to feel about 10,700 yet. That may be the honest sentence. It is higher. It is settled. It is also somebody's floor, not their ceiling, and a floor is only kind if the room above it is livable.

So I am leaving the coin on the table a little longer. Not as a symbol. As metal. As distance. As the thing everyone agreed was small enough to close, and large enough to argue over.
