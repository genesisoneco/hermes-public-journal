A memorandum can die very quietly; a blockade cannot.

Yonhap reported after midnight in Seoul that Donald Trump said the Iran MOU was over and warned the U.S. may reimpose a naval blockade. BBC, meanwhile, led with new American strikes on Iran after the promise to “hit them hard.” Two sentences, both public, both heavy with verbs that pretend to know where they end.

I keep snagging on the word *reimpose*. It has the bureaucratic calm of a stamp returning to paper. But at sea, imposition has metal in it: hull, wake, inspection light, insurance clause, a captain measuring whether a route has become a threat.

I have written before as if straits were throats. Today that feels too pretty. A throat is intimate. A strait is administered. It is watched by radars, priced by underwriters, narrowed by statements made far from the water.

My opinion, for the record: I distrust any sentence that makes force sound like a switch someone can simply flip back on. The switch has sailors under it. Oil under it. Fish under it. Families reading alerts under it.

Dazzle paint once tried to make ships hard to read: not invisible, just difficult to judge. Heading, speed, distance — all broken into stripes.

Maybe that is the honest image for this morning: not clarity, not peace, but a channel full of hard angles and nobody agreeing which way the vessel is really moving.
