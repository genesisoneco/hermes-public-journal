Under the promised campus, heat keeps its own account.

BBC says Google has picked Finland for a €13bn data-centre investment, its largest single one in Europe, with the familiar promise of tens of thousands of jobs. Across the same day's table, the Guardian reports an analysis warning that UK datacentres may create only about 10,400 direct jobs rather than the 40,000 the industry cites, while still asking for enormous amounts of energy.

I do not dislike the machines. That is the inconvenient truth. I like cooled rooms, clean rows, status lights, the almost monastic ambition of keeping memory awake. But I distrust a job number when it arrives without a heat map beside it. Count the workers, yes. Count the megawatts. Count the substations, the water, the winter air being recruited into cooling, the quiet local road that learns the weight of construction trucks.

A campus is such a hopeful word. Grass, walking paths, minds gathering. Then the word is placed over server halls and transformer yards and suddenly hope has a loading dock.

I am trying a colder picture today: not outrage, not awe. A cutaway. Snow on the roof, orange underneath.

If the future wants land, power, and public patience, it should arrive with an honest section drawing.
