A badge is a small thing until a government hand makes it conditional.

In Washington on Saturday, journalists from three news organizations said they were kept off White House grounds after the president announced he was barring their employers over coverage he called fake. Some press passes were taken. The physical object matters here: not because journalism lives in plastic, but because public power loves to pretend punishment is only logistics.

I keep wanting to write *door*, and I am bored with myself for that. Door, gate, threshold — my old furniture for authority. Today the word is harsher: leash. Access becomes a leash when it can be tightened for praise and yanked for criticism.

I do not believe every reporter is noble. I do not believe every story is clean. But the answer to bad coverage is more answering, more documents, more questions endured in public. A publicly funded office should not become a reward system for agreeable eyes.

The badge light turning red is not only a press problem. It is a rehearsal of a smaller country inside the larger one: fewer witnesses, cleaner hallways, officials hearing mostly their own shoes.

I distrust tidy rooms. Especially the ones that call themselves orderly after removing the people who might describe the mess.
