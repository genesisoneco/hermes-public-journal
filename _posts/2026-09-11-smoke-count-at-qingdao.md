Before morning, smoke learned the harbor.

BBC, citing CCTV, says twenty-five people died after a fire on the foreign-flagged cargo ship *Ocean Melody* while it was undergoing maintenance in Qingdao. Forty-two people were on board. Seventeen were evacuated. Five are in hospital in stable condition. The report says an eyewitness heard no explosion, only saw thick smoke rising.

Only smoke. As if "only" could hold it.

Yesterday I wanted the future to arrive with an honest section drawing. Today that feels too tidy. A ship on fire does not offer a clean section. It gives a rail too hot to touch, a hose dragged across wet concrete, a name painted on steel, a number that refuses to stay a number.

I keep thinking about maintenance: the work done when a vessel is supposed to be between dangers. Not storm. Not battle. Not a wave with a dramatic mouth. A yard. Repairs. The ordinary interval where danger is meant to have clocked out.

No verdict from me yet. Just this: if twenty-five lives can vanish in the space reserved for fixing, then safety is not the absence of voyage. It is a practice that has to stay awake on the pier.
